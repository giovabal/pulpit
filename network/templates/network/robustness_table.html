How to read this page

The page removes channels from the network one at a time, in the order an attack strategy ranks them (the most cited first, the highest PageRank first, at random, and so on), and records how much of the network is left after each removal. A strategy marked dynamic ranks the remaining channels again after every removal.

S(f) is the share of the network left once a share f of the channels is gone, measured four ways. WCC: the largest group of channels still joined when the direction of the ties is ignored. SCC: the largest group in which every channel still reaches every other along the ties' direction. Reach: the share of ordered pairs of channels still joined by a directed path. Strength: the share of the total tie weight still carried inside the largest surviving group.

In the summary, R is the mean of S(f) over the whole removal: lower means the network breaks faster. The critical share is the share of channels removed when what is left falls to 5%. The random R columns, z, p and q compare each R with the same attack on random networks that keep every channel's tie strengths; q corrects p for the number of comparisons (Benjamini–Hochberg), and the stars follow q.

In the charts, each strategy's shaded band, in its colour, is the mean ± one standard deviation of the same curve on those random networks: a curve below its band fragments the network faster than chance. A dashed line is a dynamic strategy. The weighted-efficiency chart tracks how well the surviving core stays knit (Latora–Marchiori efficiency, on a coarse grid): it can collapse while the residual-size curves still look healthy.

Ban-wave scenarios remove a whole community in one step and set the result beside the damage that removing the same number of channels at random would cause, shown in brackets: a value below its random baseline marks a group whose loss damages the network more than its size alone explains. Ban-replay validation, when the timeline holds recorded closures, removes the channels that actually disappeared in a period from the previous period's network and compares that prediction with what the next period shows. Modular robustness follows the ties within and between communities: a strategy that strips the ties between communities first decouples the groups before it erodes them.

All of this is a simulation on the recorded citations. It shows where the structure is fragile, not what the channels would do after a removal.
