How to read this page

A community is a group of channels tied to each other more than to the rest. Each section is one way of grouping the channels: a detection method that reads the citations, or one of the analyst's label groups.

For every community the table gives its size, the ties inside it and across its boundary, and how closed it is: the E-I index runs from −1, every tie inside, to +1, every tie outside. Density, reciprocity and clustering describe the ties inside; path length and diameter, how many steps separate its channels.

Modularity Q, above each table, says how much more the ties stay inside the communities than chance would give. Where a z is shown, it compares Q with random networks in which every channel cites and is cited as much as it really does: a high Q with a z near zero is no evidence of communities.

Under each table come the list of channels and the overlap of the communities with each label group. With a timeline, the flow diagram follows the channels from one period's communities into the next.

Partition comparison scores the agreement between the methods, and Community intersection shows which communities of two methods share channels.

The methods answer different questions and none of them is the true grouping: one that several methods agree on is better supported than one a single method finds.
