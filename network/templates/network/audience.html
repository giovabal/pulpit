How to read this page

Telegram shows a channel's subscribers only to its admins, so the part of the audience that can be followed person by person is the commenters: the user accounts replying in the observed channels' discussion groups, to posts inside the channels' observed periods and the export's dates. Telegram bots and bulk posters are left out.

Commenters are a self-selected minority of any audience, and only the channels with comments are described, not the whole network's audience. Aggregates only: no account is named.

Participation inequality

How unevenly the replies are spread over the accounts writing them: a comment section can look like a crowd while a few regulars write most of it.

Breadth

Over how many channels, and across how many labels, each commenter spreads — against what chance would give, since an account that writes more replies reaches more channels anyway (rarefaction; Hill number of order 1 across labels).

Audience overlap

Which channels share commenters beyond chance: two big channels share many anyway, so each pair is tested against a null that keeps every commenter's number of channels exactly (exact p-values, Benjamini–Hochberg q over every pair).

Retention

Whether the comment audience renews itself or rests on a core that ages: commenters per month, and how much of each quarter's newcomers comes back.

Channels as commenters

Comments written by a channel under its own name. On another channel's posts, that is a channel-to-channel tie the citation network — built from forwards and mentions — does not see. Channels are public actors, so they are named here.
