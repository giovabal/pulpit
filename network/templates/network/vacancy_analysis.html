How to read this page

A vacancy is a channel that stopped publishing on a known date. The channels that used to forward it are left without a source: the page looks for the channels those orphaned amplifiers turned to afterwards, and scores each one as a possible replacement.

The candidates are the channels the orphaned amplifiers forwarded in the months after the closure. Each score runs from 0 to 1 and weighs a different kind of evidence: whether the same channels now amplify the candidate, whether they took it up only after the closure, whether it cites and is cited by the same channels as the vacancy, whether it sits between the same groups, and whether it republishes what the vacancy published.

A q column says whether an overlap is larger than chance gives for channels this active (hypergeometric test, corrected for the number of candidates with Benjamini–Hochberg); the stars follow q.

Where the analyst recorded the known successor of a vacancy, its row is marked, and the first table reports how often each score ranked that successor first, in the top three or in the top five.

A high score shows that a channel took the position the vacancy left. It does not show that the same people run it.
